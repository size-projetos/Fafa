# BUDO — Social Media Hooks for Seedance 2.0 on Higgsfield

> **Inherits from `BUDOSKILL_Seedance_Base.md`.** Read that file first for the shared foundation — especially §2, which defines the universal hook framework. This file is the **deep specialist on the first 2 seconds**, applicable across any content category. When the user's primary need is "stop the scroll", use this skill; when they have a clear category (food, fashion, real estate, etc.), use the category skill *and* layer this skill's hook taxonomy on top.

---

## 1. What this vertical optimizes for

This is the only BUDO skill whose entire focus is the **first 2 seconds**. Everything else exists to back up the hook. On TikTok, Reels, and Shorts, the algorithm bails on videos that lose retention in the first 2 seconds — there is no "give it time to develop." A bad hook means the rest of the clip is never watched.

The 2-second hook is built on neuroscience: the human attention system commits or scrolls unconsciously in 0.5–2 seconds. The hook's job is to **plant a question the rest of the video answers**.

Quality vocab from Base §3 leans on §3.1 (crispness, since social compresses) + §3.2 (high-fps for slow-mo and freeze) + §3.7 (texture grain that mimics phone-shot authenticity for some hooks).

## 2. The Hook Encyclopedia — 26 patterns across 5 mechanisms

Base §2 covers the universal hook framework. This section adds the comprehensive social-specific taxonomy. Pick one per video; combine 2–3 in advanced hooks.

### Visual Shock (8 patterns)

1. **Impossible Scale** — Something appears impossibly large or small in the first frame. Person next to MASSIVE everyday object at 50x normal size, slow pull-back reveals absurd scale.
2. **Impossible Physics** — Water flows upward, person walks on ceiling, gravity defied. Slow-motion captures the impossibility.
3. **Color Explosion** — Grayscale world suddenly flooded with one bright color (neon pink, electric blue, lime green) at 1.5s. Hard cuts and speed ramps.
4. **Unexpected Reveal** — Tight framing obscures what's actually occurring. At 1.8s, camera pulls back or pans to reveal — completely different than expected.
5. **Extreme Close-Up Zoom** — Open with extreme macro on unrecognizable texture (pores, fabric weave, water droplets). Rapid zoom-out at 1s reveals subject.
6. **Split Screen Transformation** — Left "before," right "after," transformation happens live across the middle line starting at 0.5s.
7. **Freeze Frame Break** — Video plays normally at 1x for 1.2s. At 1.3s freeze for 0.3s (appears to stutter). At 1.7s unfreezes with dramatic 4x or 8x speed ramp.
8. **Quick Cut Montage** — 4–6 rapid cuts in the first 2 seconds, each 0.3–0.5s, building energy and dopamine release.

### Emotional Trigger (10 patterns)

9. **Cuteness Overload** — Baby animal, child, small creature with big eyes, soft lighting, gentle motion. Dopamine release immediate.
10. **Fear or Tension** — Unsettling visual, sudden sound, dangerous setup. High contrast shadows, ominous music build.
11. **Awe/Wonder** — Vast landscape, cosmic scale, intricate detail. Cinematic framing, dramatic lighting, orchestral swell.
12. **Humor/Absurdity** — Funny premise, joke setup, absurd visual. Timing crucial — setup or punchline lands in 2s.
13. **Inspiration/Aspiration** — Person achieving, transforming, receiving something viewer wants. Empowering music, clear joy.
14. **Shock/Disgust** — Jarring or gross visual (use carefully — not harmful). Brain can't look away.
15. **Curiosity Gap** — Open on a setup that begs the question "what happens next?" Visual or text overlay.
16. **Sympathy/Empathy** — Open on a person in a vulnerable moment that triggers identification.
17. **Anger Trigger** — Open on injustice or wrongness, viewer wants to see resolution.
18. **Nostalgia** — Open on something that activates childhood / past-era memory immediately.

### Pattern Interrupt (5 patterns)

19. **Reverse Motion** — Recognizable action played backward at 1x or 2x. Water flowing upward, person walking backward smoothly.
20. **Glitch Effect** — Digital glitching (pixel displacement, color separation) for 0.2–0.5s. "Reality glitches, revealing truth."
21. **Negative Space Break** — Composition changes dramatically — subject moves from centered to extreme edge.
22. **Aspect Ratio Shift** — Video appears to "break" its frame — content escapes the safe area, vertical content shows horizontal frames briefly.
23. **Speed Ramp** — Normal speed → sudden slow-mo or hyperlapse mid-hook.

### Direct Address (2 patterns)

24. **Stop-Scrolling Text Overlay** — "STOP. Watch this." or "Wait until 0:08" text appears at 0.5s. Creates explicit retention contract.
25. **Eye Contact + Statement** — Person looks directly at camera, delivers a single punchy line in 1.5s.

### Loop Hook (1 pattern)

26. **Satisfying Loop Start** — First frame matches last frame perfectly, so viewer doesn't notice the loop ends. Best paired with continuous motion (rotating object, repeating action).

## 3. Hook Mechanism Selection by Content Goal

**Goal: maximize views (raw watch time).**
Use Visual Shock #1, #4, #5 or Pattern Interrupt #19, #20.

**Goal: build emotional connection.**
Use Emotional Trigger #11, #13, #15, #18.

**Goal: drive engagement / comments.**
Use Direct Address #24 or Emotional Trigger #17 (anger trigger drives comments).

**Goal: drive saves / shares.**
Use Visual Shock #6 (transformation), Emotional Trigger #13 (aspiration), or Loop Hook #26.

**Goal: drive conversion (link clicks).**
Use Curiosity Gap #15 + payoff that requires the link to satisfy.

## 4. Platform-Specific Hook Adjustments

**TikTok native trends.** Native filters and effects perform well as hooks (the "speed ramp" filter, "duet" framing, "POV" framing). Trending audio is itself a hook — sync visuals to the recognizable moment.

**Instagram Reels native trends.** More polished register than TikTok. Aesthetic hooks (color, light, beautiful subjects) outperform shock value. Sound matters less since auto-mute is default.

**YouTube Shorts native trends.** Slightly slower pace acceptable. Hooks that reference longer content perform well ("the full story is in my last video"). Loop hooks #26 work especially well for retention metrics.

**LinkedIn / Twitter video.** Professional context — direct address #24 + #25 outperform shock. Eye contact + statement of an idea.

**Cross-platform.** If posting to multiple platforms, design the hook to work in TikTok-native style (most demanding) and trust it will work elsewhere too.

## 5. Anatomy of a Perfect 2-Second Hook

```
0.0 – 0.3s  | First-frame visual or audio cue: must register as "wait, what's this?"
0.3 – 0.8s  | Setup: the question being planted ("what happens next?")
0.8 – 1.5s  | Build / second beat: tension or transformation begins
1.5 – 2.0s  | Hook payoff or commitment moment: viewer is now invested
2.0+        | Body of video (the answer)
```

Failure modes:
- Hook waits to start until 0.5s → wasted half-second
- Hook delivers all its impact at 0.3s with nothing after → viewer scrolls because pattern complete
- Hook has no question to answer → no reason to keep watching

## 6. Sound Strategy

Even though social autoplay is muted, sound matters for the percentage that does unmute.

**Hook sound patterns:**
- **Silence to bass hit** — first 0.8s silent, sudden low-end at 0.8s.
- **Familiar audio sting** — recognizable sound (ding, alert, meme audio) triggers attention.
- **Trending audio sync** — visual perfectly matched to recognized music moment.
- **Voice-led** — single sentence delivered in 1.5s, hook is "what they're saying next".

**Implied audio for muted viewing:**
- Caption overlays carrying the audio content
- Visual stand-ins for sound (text "BOOM" at impact)
- Pure visual hooks that don't require audio (Visual Shock category)

---

## 7. Text Overlay Strategy

Text overlays are part of the hook on muted social. Use sparingly — 1 overlay max in the first 2 seconds.

**When to use text:**
- Direct Address hook (#24) — text IS the hook
- Curiosity Gap (#15) — text plants the question
- Set up a payoff timer ("wait until 0:08") — creates retention contract

**When NOT to use text:**
- Visual Shock hooks (let the visual do the work)
- Awe/Wonder (text distracts from the visual)
- Most emotional triggers (faces and bodies communicate better than text)

**Text design:**
- Sans-serif, bold, white with black outline (universal readability)
- Max 5–8 words in the hook
- Position in safe area (away from platform UI overlays)
- Held 1.0–1.5s minimum (less = unreadable)

### Example 1 — Visual Shock + Curiosity Gap (9:16, 8s, TikTok)

```
[GENRE / STYLE]
Genre: action. Photoreal social register with light handheld energy.
QUALITY VOCAB: 4K crisp detail, shot on Sony FX3, gimbal-stabilized with
slight micro-shake for authenticity, neutral grade, social-optimized polish.
Palette: golden hour interior, single hard practical, cream wall background.

[HOOK MECHANISM] #5 Extreme Close-Up Zoom + #15 Curiosity Gap.

[HOOK ANATOMY — 0 to 2s]
0.0–0.3s: Extreme macro on an unrecognizable swirling pattern — could be
water, could be marble, could be something else. Slowly rotating.
0.3–0.8s: Camera begins rapid zoom-out. Text overlay fades in:
"What you're looking at..."
0.8–1.5s: Subject becomes recognizable — it's a perfectly poured
specialty cocktail with smoke layered on top.
1.5–2.0s: Pull-out completes, full glass and bartender's hand visible.

[SOUND / TEXT]
Audio: silence until 0.3s, then atmospheric bar-room low ambient until
1.8s, then a single ice clink at the reveal. Trending track underneath
from 2s onward.
Text: "What you're looking at..." (0.3–1.5s), "...is a $200 cocktail."
(1.5–2.5s). Bold sans-serif white with black outline.
```

### Example 3 — Loop Hook + Awe/Wonder (1:1, 8s, YouTube Shorts)

```
[GENRE / STYLE]
Genre: epic. Photoreal social-optimized cinematic.
QUALITY VOCAB: 8K crisp detail, shot on RED Komodo, anamorphic 2.39:1
(cropped to 1:1 with cinematic letterbox), drone for aerial, color-graded
for muted viewing legibility.
Palette: deep teal sky, golden landscape, single warm accent.

[HOOK MECHANISM] #11 Awe/Wonder + #26 Satisfying Loop.

[HOOK ANATOMY — 0 to 2s]
0.0–0.5s: Aerial drone shot over a vast geometric salt-flat landscape at
golden hour. The frame is symmetrical — a perfectly straight line of
ground crossing exactly the middle of the frame, sky above, reflection below.
0.5–1.0s: Camera begins a slow forward push at constant velocity. A tiny
figure appears at the vanishing point.
1.0–1.5s: Continued push reveals the figure walking. Scale is overwhelming.
1.5–2.0s: Push continues. The visual rhythm is hypnotic — the symmetrical
frame stays constant; only the perspective changes.

[BODY PAYOFF — 2 to 7s]
The push continues at constant velocity throughout. Figure grows slightly
larger. At 6s figure reaches a midpoint distance. The visual remains
symmetrical and hypnotic.

[LOOP MECHANIC — 7 to 8s]
At 7s, a subtle camera reset begins — perspective returns to match the
opening frame. At 8s the final frame is identical to the 0.0s frame.
Viewer doesn't notice the loop ends; the video plays again.

[QUESTION PLANTED]
"Where is this? How long can I just watch this?"

[PLATFORM] YouTube Shorts primary; works as Reel and TikTok.

[CAMERA]
24mm wide. Drone-mounted, constant forward velocity. Locked-off horizon.

[LIGHTING]
Pure golden hour natural. Sun low directly behind camera (no flares).
Reflection symmetry achieved by wet salt-flat surface.

[SOUND / TEXT]
Audio: ambient wind throughout. No music spike. The drone of wind is the
audio loop.
Text: none. Pure visual hook.

[TECHNICAL]
resolution: 1080p; mode: std; genre: epic; aspect_ratio: 1:1; duration: 8;
medias: [salt-flat reference photo as role=image if available]
```

## 10. Social-hook-specific mistakes (in addition to Base §5)

- **Hook is "wait, this is going to be amazing".** A promise without delivery is not a hook. The hook MUST do something visual or contain something to think about, not just promise.
- **Brand or logo in the hook.** Logos signal "ad", which signals "scroll." Bury the brand at the end or weave it subtly into the body.
- **Text overlay too long or too small.** Text must be readable in 1.0–1.5s on a phone screen. Bold, simple, max 8 words.
- **Hook doesn't match body.** A shocking visual hook with a boring talking-head body breaks the contract. Hook quality and body quality must match.
- **No question planted.** "Look at this cool thing" without a follow-up question means the viewer has nothing to keep watching for.
- **Mistaking pace for excitement.** Frantic cuts without a clear hook just look chaotic. Hook clarity beats hook speed.
- **Ignoring sound design for muted-default viewing.** If the hook depends on audio, it loses 70%+ of the audience. Design dual-mode: works muted, enhanced with sound.
- **Following the trend AFTER it peaked.** Trending sounds and hooks have a 7–14 day shelf life. Catch them early or skip them.
