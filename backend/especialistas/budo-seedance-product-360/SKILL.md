# BUDO — Product 360° Showcase Videos for Seedance 2.0 on Higgsfield

> **Inherits from `BUDOSKILL_Seedance_Base.md`.** Read that file first for the shared foundation. This file adds only what's **specific to 360° product showcase / turntable / multi-angle visualization.** Distinct from `BUDOSKILL_ProductAd.md` — that one is for *advertising* (conversion); this one is for *showcasing* (information).

---

## 1. What this vertical optimizes for

Product 360° video has a different goal than product ads. The 360° showcase exists to **inform the buyer** — let them see every angle, every material, every detail, so they can make a purchase decision with confidence. It's e-commerce listing content, not scroll-stopping social content.

Where ProductAd wants emotion and conversion, Product360 wants **clarity, completeness, and trust**.

Quality vocab from Base §3 leans on §3.1 (every-detail sharpness), §3.4 (macro lens), §3.5 (clean studio lighting), and §3.3 (path-traced reflections for luxury items).

---

## 2. Product showcase hook library (12 hooks)

1. **Particle Materialization** — Product emerges from a cloud of particles or dust, slowly solidifying. Premium register.
2. **Dramatic Spotlight Snap** — Single harsh spotlight suddenly illuminates the product in darkness. Luxury watch/jewelry staple.
3. **Crash & Shockwave** — Product crashes down from above with impact, subtle dust/motion blur. Implies robustness.
4. **Unwrap / Peel Reveal** — Packaging peels away, tissue unfolds, protective layer retracts. Unboxing energy.
5. **Impossible-Angle Macro-to-Wide** — Start extreme close-up (micro detail), pull out to full product view.
6. **Self-Assembly** — Product components float, snap into place. Tech/modular products.
7. **Exploded View Snap-Together** — All parts visible and separated, then snap together. Complexity narrative.
8. **Liquid Pour / Transparency Bloom** — Transparent product fills with color/liquid. Beauty/beverage.
9. **Slow-Motion Lift** — Product gracefully lifts from surface in slow motion, floating into rotation.
10. **Spin-to-Reveal** — Half rotation (180°) reveals hidden side detail, embossing, branding.
11. **Zoom & Pan Transition** — Camera zooms into texture, then pans to full product. Material storytelling.
12. **Halo Light Flare** — Light reflection or flare appears around product. Jewelry default.

---

## 3. Product Showcase Philosophy

**Completeness over excitement.** A buyer needs to see the front, back, sides, top, bottom, key details, scale. Cover all of them.

**Clean is professional.** Busy backgrounds undermine trust. Solid white, soft gradient, or premium-context environment only.

**Rotation reveals; cuts inform.** Smooth rotation reveals form; sharp cuts to detail shots reveal specifics. Use both.

**Lighting is the lens through which trust is built.** Hard light = dramatic / luxury. Soft light = approachable / everyday. Mixed = premium-but-accessible.

**Pace matches the price.** Luxury items rotate slowly (12–15s per 360°). Everyday items rotate faster (4–6s per 360°). Speed signals positioning.

---

## 4. Product Category Lighting Guide

**Jewelry / Diamonds / Gold.** Hard directional key, dark background, 6500K. Specular highlights are the demo. Path-traced reflections essential.

**Watches.** 5400K cool key from above-left, subtle fill, strong rim light. Reflections "dance" on polished surfaces.

**Sneakers / Footwear.** 3500K warm key from above, fill from below to catch sole tread. White platform. Medium rotation pace.

**Electronics.** 5000K neutral lighting, multiple soft sources, no harsh shadows. Screen content needs to be readable.

**Beauty / Skincare.** 5500K cool from above for white-balanced skin tone neutrality, soft fill. White or pastel background.

**Furniture / Home.** 4500K daylight from one direction, fill from opposite. Show in scale context (room corner).

**Fashion accessories (bags, belts).** Warm 3500K, single directional light, dark or wood background. Texture is the demo.

**Food (packaged).** Daylight 5500K from above-back, soft fill. Steam/dew effects implied.

**Tools / Hardware.** Mixed temperature for color depth, hard directional to show machining marks. Industrial backdrop optional.

**Toys / Collectibles.** Vibrant lighting, slight warm bias, white or themed background.

---

## 5. Rotation & Camera Movement Library

**Full 360° rotation.** Object rotates around vertical axis. Standard for most categories.

**Half 360° (180°) reveal.** Front-to-back arc only. Best when product has clear "front" face.

**Triple-axis tumble.** Three rotation axes blended. Best for compact objects like gadgets, jewelry. Reveals form fully.

**Orbital camera (object static).** Camera orbits a stationary product. Smoother for asymmetric products that look awkward on turntables.

**Push-in / Pull-out.** Camera moves toward or away from product. Adds drama or context shift.

**Lateral track.** Camera slides past product. Editorial-feeling.

**Tilt reveal.** Camera tilts down or up to reveal product progressively.

**Static hold.** Used between rotations for detail beats. Critical — don't only rotate.

---

## 6. Material & Surface Showcase Techniques

**Metal (polished).** Hard light from one direction; specular highlights move with rotation.
**Metal (brushed).** Light from oblique angle to catch the grain direction.
**Glass / crystal.** Backlight + side light. Caustics from refraction are the demo.
**Leather.** Soft directional light + low-angle fill to show grain. Avoid hard specular.
**Plastic (matte).** Diffuse light, no harsh shadows.
**Plastic (glossy).** Hard light, controlled specular position.
**Fabric (smooth — silk, satin).** Light along the grain to show drape.
**Fabric (textured — wool, knit).** Side light to catch fiber dimension.
**Wood.** Warm light, low angle to show grain.
**Gemstone.** Multiple hard sources to maximize light entering the stone; show fire (spectral dispersion).
**Skincare jars (frosted glass).** Backlit to show contents through frosted exterior.
**Ceramic / porcelain.** Soft light from multiple sources; glaze sheen on highlight side only.

---

## 7. Background & Environment Library

**Pure white seamless.** Default e-commerce. Maximum focus on product.
**Pure black seamless.** Luxury / drama. Specular highlights pop.
**Soft gradient (cool blue to white).** Tech / electronics. Modern.
**Soft gradient (warm cream to peach).** Beauty / skincare. Approachable.
**Marble surface.** Luxury / jewelry / watches. Cool tones.
**Wood surface.** Food / craft / artisan. Warm tones.
**Concrete / Industrial.** Tech / tools / streetwear. Cool tones.
**Linen / Fabric drape.** Fashion / accessories. Texture richness.
**Nature setting (flat ground in forest, beach, snow).** Outdoor gear, lifestyle.
**Themed studio set.** Product-specific (kitchen counter for kitchenware, gym for athletic wear).


## 10. Product360-specific mistakes (in addition to Base §5)

- **Rotation only, no detail beats.** A 360° rotation alone tells the buyer the product exists, not what makes it special. Always intersperse detail shots.
- **Wrong pace for the price point.** $50 product rotating in 15s reads as overpriced posturing. $5,000 product rotating in 4s reads as bargain-bin treatment. Match pace to price.
- **Busy background.** Backgrounds compete for attention. Use Library §7 — solid or simple gradient by default.
- **No reference photo.** Product showcase is the highest-stakes vertical for identity preservation. Always pass the product photo via `medias`.
- **Confusing showcase with ad.** If you find yourself writing CTAs and aspirational lifestyle, you're making a ProductAd — use that skill instead.
- **Speed inconsistency.** Rotation that speeds up/slows down arbitrarily looks broken. Smooth constant velocity is the norm.
- **Detail shots that don't reveal anything new.** If a detail beat shows the same surface as the rotation showed, it's filler. Each detail should reveal something the rotation can't.
