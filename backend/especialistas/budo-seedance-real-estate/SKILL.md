## 1. What this vertical optimizes for

Real estate video sells **space, light, and lifestyle** — three intangibles that photos struggle to capture but that move buyers. A successful property video answers: *"Could I live here? What's the daily experience? How does this space feel?"*

Override on Base §6: real estate has unique destination characteristics. Zillow/Realtor.com accept up to 5-minute video tours; Instagram property reels need 9:16 + 15–60s; YouTube property walkthroughs work as 16:9 + 2–10 min. The hook window can extend to 0–2.5s for property listings (vs 0–1s for social) since these viewers have already clicked.

Quality vocab from Base §3 is essential — real estate buys premium register signals (8K + Steadicam + Architectural Digest polish).

## 2. Real estate hook library (12 hooks)

1. **Drone Aerial Swoop Toward Property** — Start high above, revealing context (neighborhood, landscape, location). Swoop downward toward facade. *Best for: homes with impressive grounds, skyline views.*
2. **Door Opening to Reveal Stunning Interior** — Shot from inside looking out (or vice versa). Heavy door swings open to soaring ceilings, light-flooded rooms. *Universally compelling.*
3. **Window View That Takes Your Breath Away** — Pan across room, find the window, approach until landscape/cityscape fills frame. *Best for: waterfront, mountain views, skyline apartments.*
4. **Before/After Renovation Flash** — Quick cut between original dilapidated space and renovated showpiece. *Best for: fixer-uppers turned luxury.*
5. **Light Flooding Into Dark Space** — Begin in shadow; door/window opens flooding space with golden or bright light. *Best for: dramatic skylights, south-facing windows, new construction.*
6. **Infinity Pool Edge With Landscape Beyond** — Water-level shot toward edge; pool blends with ocean/valley/cityscape. *Best for: luxury homes, resorts, premium outdoor spaces.*
7. **Architectural Detail Extreme Close-Up** — Impossibly close to detail (marble veining, wood joinery, tile craftsmanship), pull back to full room. *Best for: high-end designer homes.*
8. **Day-to-Night Transition** — Property in daylight transitions to twilight + evening lighting. Interior lights come alive. *Best for: urban properties, 24-hour lifestyle.*
9. **Front Door Approach** — Slow steady approach down driveway/walkway toward entrance. Builds anticipation. *Foundational hook, all property types.*
10. **Skyline from Penthouse Window** — Shot from window over urban landscape. Communicates prestige and height. *Best for: high-rise apartments, penthouses.*
11. **Negative Space & Light Play** — Room defined by light and shadow. Geometric light rectangles move across floors. *Best for: modern, contemporary, minimal properties.*
12. **Material Transformation Under Light** — A surface (marble, wood, glass, concrete) shifts in appearance as light moves across it. *Best for: statement-finish properties.*

## 3. Real Estate Video Philosophy: Space as Character

**Space is the protagonist.** The buyer isn't buying square footage; they're buying the feeling of standing in those rooms. Cinematography must convey that feeling.

**Light is the language of luxury.** Hard light = stark drama. Soft light = approachability. Mixed natural + practical = lived-in warmth. The same room in different light reads as different price points.

**Movement reveals scale.** Static shots fail to communicate room size and ceiling height. Slow camera movement (push-in, dolly, crane) does what photos can't.

**Restraint signals quality.** Music swells, fast cuts, and excessive transitions read as discount real estate. High-end property video moves slowly.

**Lead with the wow.** Whatever is most exceptional about the property — view, ceiling, architecture, location — should be in the first 5 seconds.

## 4. Property Type Guide

**Luxury single-family (waterfront / view).** Slow drone approach, golden hour, anamorphic, Steadicam interior glides. Multi-clip 60–120s.

**Urban penthouse / high-rise apartment.** POV from window outward, then pull back to reveal interior. Skyline at twilight is the hero. 9:16 for social, 16:9 for hero.

**Modern minimalist contemporary.** Negative space + light play, geometric framing, soft natural light, restraint in editing.

**Traditional family home.** Warm lived-in lighting, family-context implied (kid's bedroom, kitchen island), approachable pace.

**Mid-century modern.** Architectural detail focus, period-appropriate styling, hard directional light for the architecture.

**New construction.** Bright clean lighting, every detail crisp, slight CGI render-clean look, slow camera movement showing finishes.

**Fixer-upper / Investment property.** Before/after framing, honest about current state but optimistic about potential.

**Vacation rental / Airbnb.** Lifestyle-led, hosts/guests implied through styling (set table, coffee on counter), warmer pace.

**Commercial / Office.** Daylight workspace energy, people implied through styling (laptop on desk, plants), 16:9 corporate.

**Land / Lot.** Drone-heavy, context-establishing, often no interior to show.

## 5. Room-by-Room Showcase Strategy

**Entrance / Foyer.** Establishes arrival. Show ceiling height, light source, sight-line through to next room.

**Living Room.** Often the hero room. Feature: fireplace if present, ceiling height, window framing.

**Kitchen.** Functional + aspirational. Counter material, appliance brand visible, island as social hub.

**Dining Room.** Lifestyle implied. Show seating capacity and view from table.

**Master Bedroom.** Intimacy. Show ceiling, window, walk-in closet glimpse.

**Master Bathroom.** Spa register. Tub or shower hero, vanity material, light source.

**Secondary Bedrooms.** Brief — show flexibility (office potential, kid's room potential).

**Home Office.** Increasingly important — show window light, ceiling, space for setup.

**Outdoor.** Patio/deck/pool as extension of living space. Show in context to interior.

**Garage / Storage.** Quick — show capacity, organization potential.

## 6. Camera Movement Library for Spaces

**Drone aerial.** Establishes context, scale. Approach/orbit/recede.

**Steadicam glide.** Smooth walking-pace camera through rooms. Most common interior movement.

**Slider / dolly.** Smooth lateral movement past a feature. Often used past kitchen island or living room sofa.

**Crane / jib.** Vertical rise reveals ceiling height. Or fall reveals floor + spatial depth.

**Push-in.** Reveals the view through a window — slow approach to glass.

**Pull-out.** Reveals scale — starts on detail, pulls to show full room.

**Orbit / circle.** Camera circles a central feature (fireplace, kitchen island).

**Tilt up / down.** Reveals ceiling or floor material.

**Static hold.** Wide composed frame; let the space speak.

---

## 7. Lighting & Time-of-Day Guide

**Morning (8–10am).** Cool 5000K east-facing light. Crisp, fresh, energetic. Good for kitchen, breakfast nook.

**Midday.** Bright neutral. Best avoided unless overcast — direct sun creates harsh shadows.

**Afternoon (3–4pm).** Warm 4000K. Soft balanced light. Universal good time.

**Golden hour (5–7pm depending on season).** Warm 3000K, long shadows. Best for exterior and west-facing rooms. Most premium register.

**Blue hour (twilight, 7–8pm).** Deep blue sky with warm interior light glowing. The luxury real estate look. Day-to-night transition lands here.

**Night with interior lights.** Warm interior glow against dark sky. Urban/penthouse register.

**Overcast natural.** Soft directional with no harsh shadows. Best for showing colors accurately.

## 10. Real-estate-specific mistakes (in addition to Base §5)

- **Camera moves too fast.** Real estate buyers want time to absorb the space. 1–3 ft/s is the range; faster than that and rooms blur together.
- **Music swells through everything.** Restraint. Build the music *into* the visual peaks (entry moment, view reveal), not throughout.
- **Wide-angle distortion misleading.** Aggressive 14mm "real estate lens" distortion oversells small spaces and reads as manipulation now that buyers know the trick. 24–35mm equivalent is honest.
- **No people implied.** Houses look abandoned without lived-in signals (coffee on counter, blanket on sofa, book on nightstand). Stage the lived-in cues.
- **All daytime.** Day-to-night transition is one of the strongest premium real estate moves. Use it at least once in a longer tour.
- **Drone everywhere.** Drone fatigue — buyers see every property opening with a drone aerial. Vary the opening; sometimes start inside, sometimes start with detail.
- **Identity confusion.** Always pass property photos via `medias` to preserve actual property identity. A "generic luxury home" doesn't sell THIS home.
